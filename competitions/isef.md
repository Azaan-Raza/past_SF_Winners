# Regeneron ISEF (International Science and Engineering Fair)

The world final. About 1,700 finalists a year, selected through affiliated regional and state fairs. Our route is ACSEF (Alameda County) directly, or ACSEF to CSEF to ISEF. ISEF 2027 is May 8-14 at the Los Angeles Convention Center.

## How it works

- **Categories.** About 22 categories. Ours is Computational Biology and Bioinformatics (CBIO), which had 92 projects in 2026. Subcategories: Computational Biomodeling (MOD), Computational Epidemiology (EPD), Computational Evolutionary Biology (EVO, explicitly includes ecology), Computational Neuroscience (NEU), Computational Pharmacology (PHA), Genomics (GEN), Other (OTH). Source: [CBIO category page](https://www.societyforscience.org/isef/categories-and-subcategories/computational-biology-bioinformatics/).
- **Grand Awards per category (2026 amounts).** 1st $6,000, 2nd $2,400, 3rd $1,200, 4th $600. Several projects share each tier; in CBIO 2026 there were 2 firsts, 5 seconds, 8 thirds, 10 fourths out of 92.
- **Top awards (2026).** George D. Yancopoulos Innovator Award $75,000 (top), Regeneron Young Scientist Awards $50,000 (two), Gordon E. Moore Award $50,000, Craig R. Barrett Award for Innovation, H. Robert Horvitz Prize for Fundamental Research, Mary Sue Coleman Award, Peggy Scripps Award for Science Communication, Dudley R. Herschbach SIYSS Award (three; trip to Stockholm). Before 2021 the Gordon E. Moore Award ($75,000) was the top prize and Intel Foundation Young Scientist Awards were $50,000.
- **Judging.** 100 points: Research Question 10, Design and Methodology 15, Execution (data, analysis, interpretation) 20, Creativity and Potential Impact 20, Presentation 35 (poster 10, interview 25). Team members are each judged on contribution and understanding. See [`../analysis/judging-criteria.md`](../analysis/judging-criteria.md).
- **Rules that bite computational projects.** AI may be used as a cited resource but not to write the research plan, abstract or poster; Form 2A (Student Support Disclosure) is required for every project from 2026-27. Projects that only use publicly available datasets are "Regular" (no pre-approval) at ACSEF. Human-subject or non-public data needs early SRC/IRB review. Check the current [Rules for All Projects](https://www.societyforscience.org/isef/international-rules/rules-for-all-projects/).
- **Abstract database.** Every finalist's abstract since 2014 is at [abstracts.societyforscience.org](https://abstracts.societyforscience.org/). See [`../resources/abstract-database-howto.md`](../resources/abstract-database-howto.md).

## What the CBIO winners look like (2015-2026)

- Public data is the norm, not the exception. Most CBIO placements use TCGA, GEO, GDSC/CCLE, PDB, public sequence archives or public imaging sets. Winning with public data requires a sharp question and a validation layer; see [`../analysis/what-wins.md`](../analysis/what-wins.md).
- Teams place. CBIO 1st went to a two- or three-person team in 2015, 2021, 2022 and 2025; a team took 2nd in 2026.
- Repeat years pay. Kevin Zhu (1st 2022 and 2023), Swetha Revanur (1st 2015 and 2016), Rithvik Ganesh (2nd 2021 and 2022), Ryan Park (2nd 2021 and 2022), William Wakefield (3rd 2024, 2nd 2025), Aditya Lakshminarasimhan (2nd 2024 and 2025).
- Cancer is the most common subject and the most crowded. Among 2026 CBIO placements, cancer-specific projects landed mostly in 2nd-4th; the two firsts were a PINN imaging pipeline and a plant-pathogen evolution model.
- Bay Area and Alameda County presence: Quarry Lane (Pandit, 3rd 2026), Dublin High (Gudisay and Bansal, 2nd 2025), James Logan (Ganesh, 4th 2021), Harker, Lynbrook, Homestead, Evergreen Valley, Cupertino, Menlo-Atherton, BASIS Independent Silicon Valley, Santa Teresa.
