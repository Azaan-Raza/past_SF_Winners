# Urban evolution, citizen-science data and computer vision on organisms

Everything in the catalog that bears on PD1-EVO-01 (urban heat and snail shell colour across countries, from ~164,600 GBIF image records with a student-built classifier) and PD1-EVO-02 (urban heat vs urban intensity and pigeon plumage across the world's cities, from ~383,400 GBIF image records).

## The closest precedents, in order of closeness

1. **Ashwin Sivakumar, ISEF 2019 EAEV 4th.** Citizen-science occurrences (146,457 eBird records) correlated with public environmental layers (Landsat-8, Sentinel-2, PRISM) in Google Earth Engine at one restored wetland. The same data logic as ours, at one site. A multi-city, two-continent design with a validated classifier is the upgrade.
2. **Rithika Narayan, ISEF 2020 finalist.** Hand-annotated hundreds of crowd-sourced coral photos in six classes, fine-tuned Mask R-CNN, >85% accuracy. Exactly the labeling and training workload of the snail lead. It stopped at the classifier; we must go on to the biological test.
3. **Lillian Petersen, ISEF 2018 EAEV 1st and STS 2020 1st ($250,000).** Public satellite indices, simple regression, validated in the US then tested in Africa. The "fit in one region, test in another" move is the one we should copy: fit in the Netherlands (where Kerstes et al. found the effect), test in other countries and in North American introduced populations.
4. **Benjy Firester, STS 2018 1st ($250,000).** Spatiotemporal model of late-blight spore spread from public weather and disease data. Proof that a computational ecology or epidemiology model can win STS outright.
5. **Andrew Jin, Intel STS 2015 1st Global Good ($150,000).** Machine learning on public human genomes to find adaptive mutations. Computational evolutionary biology on public data at the very top.
6. **Noah Nager, ISEF 2023 CBIO 4th.** Public land-cover layers predicting West Nile virus across the US. Computational Epidemiology subcategory, regions as units.
7. **Addison Shea, STS 2025 finalist.** Spatial statistics on 30 years of bowhead whale sighting records against ocean circulation. Observational, biased, long-term public records analyzed carefully.
8. **Dylan Bagnall and Richard Beattie, ISEF 2019 Animal Sciences 1st.** Low-cost methods for citizen scientists in bat conservation. Judges valued making data collection scalable by non-experts, which is the premise of GBIF and iNaturalist.
9. **Anika Puri, ISEF 2022 EAEV 1st and Peggy Scripps.** Spatio-temporal ML on thermal drone video to detect poachers. Conservation plus CV plus spatiotemporal modeling.
10. **Mohammed Alasmari, ISEF 2026 CBIO 1st.** Markov model of host-pathogen resistance evolution. The EVO subcategory produced a 1st in 2026.

## What the record says

- No ISEF or STS top-award project in this catalog has done urban-evolution colour analysis of snails or pigeons from citizen-science photos. The niche is open at the high-school level; the science is established (Kerstes et al. 2019 for snails; see `resources/key-papers.md`).
- Projects that stop at "I trained a classifier" do not place (Narayan, McGill year 1). Projects that use the classifier or the data to answer a biological question do (Sivakumar, Petersen, Firester, Puri).
- Animal Sciences firsts are field experiments with quantitative analysis, often simple statistics (Madan's multiple linear regression two years running; Springer's spatial disease-rate comparison; Lo's bee training with AI analysis). The CBIO EVO subcategory is where a classifier-plus-spatial-model project fits better, and where judges will press on validation, photographer bias and alternative explanations rather than on pigeon husbandry.
- Multi-year field programs compound: Estrada siblings (Moore 2021, PLNT 1st 2022, PLNT 1st 2024), Madan (ANIM 1st 2021, 2022), Manaswi family (STS 2025 finalist, ISEF 2026 ANIM 1st), McGill (ISEF 2024, Davidson 2025, STS 2026).
