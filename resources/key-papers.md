# Key papers behind our leads

Primary literature we must cite and build on. Verified from the sources linked; items marked "verify" need the DOI checked before use.

## Cancer lead (PD1-CAN-01)

- Neiswender JV, Maffa S, Brenan L, et al. **A dependency map enhanced with next-generation 3D cancer models.** Nature, 5 Aug 2026. doi:10.1038/s41586-026-10843-7. Co-senior authors Francisca Vazquez and Tsukasa Shibue (Broad). Nearly 150 3D models across 10 cancer types added to DepMap; 3D models reveal dependencies absent from 2D lines and include subtypes that grow only in 3D; growth format affected adhesion and cytoskeleton genes, medium affected lipid-metabolism genes; CDKN2A-null glioblastoma models were sensitive to CDK6 suppression; GI organoids with a pancreatic-subtype program depended on WNT genes lost in 2D lines. Authors call 2D and 3D complementary. Data at depmap.org/portal. [Broad news summary](https://www.broadinstitute.org/news/cancer-dependency-map-now-includes-next-generation-3d-cancer-models).
- Herranz-Ors C, Bhosle SG, Beck AE, et al. **A tumour-derived organoid biobank maps cancer gene dependencies.** Nature, Aug 2026. doi:10.1038/s41586-026-10830-y. 256 organoid cultures from 878 donors (colorectal, esophageal, pancreatic, stomach, ovarian); CRISPR screens in 162 cultures; thousands of dependencies linked to genomic and clinical features; organoids reflect matched tumors; pre/post-treatment pairs show resistance evolution. [Technology Networks summary](https://www.technologynetworks.com/cell-science/news/tumor-biobank-reveals-cancers-weak-spots-415386).
- Human Cancer Models Initiative companion paper (Nature, Aug 2026): 665 models across 25 cancer types from 2,780 donors. Verify citation.
- Student precedents that cite this data family: see `topics/cancer-genomics-and-model-fidelity.md`.

## Snail lead (PD1-EVO-01)

- Kerstes NAG, Breeschoten T, Kalkman VJ, Schilthuizen M. **Snail shell colour evolution in urban heat islands detected via citizen science.** Communications Biology 2, 264 (2019). doi:10.1038/s42003-019-0511-6. Dutch citizen-science (SnailSnap) photos of Cepaea nemoralis; urban snails more often yellow (lighter), consistent with thermal selection. Data: Dryad doi:10.5061/dryad.7rh631v. [PMC](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC6642149/). [Naturalis project page](https://www.naturalis.nl/en/science/educational-activities-for-students/research-projects-and-internships/urban-evolution-heat-islands-and-snail-shell-color).
- The dossier's citation scan of the 39 works citing Kerstes et al. found no multi-country or North American replication; that is the gap.
